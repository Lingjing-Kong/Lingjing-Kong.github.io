I develop **causal representation learning** to take AI beyond prediction: recovering the concepts and mechanisms behind complex data so that models can **transfer** to new environments, expose **interpretable** structure, and support precise **control**.

Many capabilities that remain unreliable, such as controlling generation, transferring to new environments, anticipating the consequences of actions, updating knowledge, and discovering scientific mechanisms, ask what would happen under a change the training data did not show. Passive data often cannot settle these interventional and counterfactual questions. Causal principles specify which data and structural simplicity make the underlying structure identifiable, and scalable machine learning learns it.

**Research keywords:** causal representation learning, identifiability, world models, compositional generalization and reasoning, interpretable and controllable generative models, AI for scientific discovery.

I joined the [NYU Center for Data Science](https://cds.nyu.edu/) in 2026 after completing my Ph.D. in Computer Science at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by [Yuejie Chi](https://yuejiechi.github.io/), [Eric P. ## Research

Each capability is a question about change: **transferability** (what stays invariant under change?), **interpretability** (what mechanisms generate the data?), and **controllability** (what if one factor changes?). My work pursues them in four regimes:

- **A. Learning from heterogeneous data.** Identifying shared and changing mechanisms across domains and modalities.
- **B. Learning from unstructured data.** Uncovering hierarchical concepts to understand and control learned models.
- **C. Generalizing beyond data.** Reasoning with learned structure to handle combinations and conditions never observed.
- **D. Learning beyond data.** Acquiring the evidence existing data lack, through interaction and experiments.

A and B recover structure from the data at hand; C and D go beyond it. My future directions are causal world models that learn by intervening and mechanism discovery for science. Behavioral evaluation of AI agents and learning systems cuts across all of these.
